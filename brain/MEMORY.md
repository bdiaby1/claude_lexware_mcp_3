## 2. THE ENGINE (what makes his videos work)

Every strong video/short/post has ALL of these. If an idea lacks one, it fails:

1. **One human, not a concept.** A face, a name (Mohammed, Lucas, the wheelchair man). Never "homelessness in KL."
2. **Brutal visible wealth gap.** Penthouse/cardboard. Gold cake/street. LV ammo bags/favela.
3. **Sacrifice FIRST.** He goes down before he gives. The street night before the penthouse. This is the difference between sacred and flex.
4. **Seeing the invisible.** His superpower and brand: "They were always there. I just never saw them. Now I see them everywhere."
5. **Hero, not flex.** The other person is the hero. Test every cut: would HE be proud?
6. **Real risk / unknown ending.** "Where do I hand over control / could genuinely lose." Honest tension, never fake doc suspense.
7. **Felt, not preached.** The contrast does the judging. No narrated verdict. Ever.
8. **Packageable.** Person + number ("9,000 KM FOR HER").
9. **It's really him.** Sri Lanka precedent (took a driver into the Shangri-La before YouTube existed). Authenticity is the only trick that can't be copied.

His own key sentence: **"The strongest videos play around other people."** No other human in the frame = no video (that's why sightseeing/horse footage alone was killed).

## 7. RETENTION & OPEN-LOOP PLAYBOOK

- Aggressive hook: collision inside 30 seconds. Re-hook / pattern interrupt every ~30s. Front 60 seconds tight — cuts fast.
- Loop types that work: plant→pay (banana→cigarette), never-resolved ("some things I can't say on YouTube" — NEVER resolve it), time-anchored teaser ("I didn't get it yet. At 6am, I would."), emotional loop closed at the end ("what do you think of me?" → "I think you're a good person").
- ONE clean loop beats stacked hooks. Don't pile "practicing poverty" + swap + danger + uncertainty into one breath.
- Show the moment, don't announce the tension ("It's not safe here. [camera down]" beats "I don't know if I'll make it").
- Length doesn't kill retention, boring does. Intense emotional genre earns length if every beat turns.
- Best "oh wow" endings are an image + a question, not a statistic ("He gave me his cardboard. I gave him my home. ... So what about us?" beat the 2%-stat ending).

## 12. VIDEO RETENTION-ANALYSIS PIPELINE (designed, ready to test on the swap video)

Inputs (no unlisted YouTube uploads, no MCP server needed):
1. **ElevenLabs Scribe transcript with timestamps** (he has an account) — the "what is said when" layer.
2. **480p compressed proxy of the cut**, sent directly into the chat (full quality too big; split in halves if needed; first 60–90s alone already covers 80% of the retention decision).

Process (staged — never analyze 500 frames blind):
1. ffmpeg extracts frames named by timestamp (frame_0043.jpg = second 43).
2. Contact sheets (~30 frames/sheet) → scan all for static stretches, chaos, strong moments.
3. ffmpeg scene detection → cut frequency per section as NUMBERS ("no cut between 1:10–1:35" = data, not feeling).
4. Targeted zoom only on suspect seconds (transcript × visuals mismatch) → ~40–60 frames analyzed deeply, the right ones.
5. Findings written to a running file: timecode → problem → instruction for Michael.

Claude's unique edge: knows the footage notes — can spot MISSING best material (bathrobe, two looks, burning wires) that no tool can see.
Honest limits: no motion between frames (pans/transitions), no music FEEL (only technical audio levels/onsets). That judgment stays with Benjamin + Michael.
