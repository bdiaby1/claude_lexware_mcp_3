## Core value points (his idea, listed)
1. Privilege is given, not earned — the country you're born in is the inheritance nobody worked for.
2. What was given isn't yours. It feels like yours — that's the trap.
3. The test: what if at the end everything is weighed by what you were given; 90x more = 90x
   more to answer for. "Ich sage nur: was wenn." Never framed as religion.
4. The one below you may be better than you (Görli: "maybe better people than you"; swap: "the
   people who have the least give the most"; Istanbul: the gloves).
5. So: don't rescue — sit, let them teach you; money is a tool to unlock small dreams for others.
6. The contrast sits on ONE man (same body, same month); the question lands on him first.

## Caption (English; every sentence his, only the joints are new)
The G-Class. The Waldorf. What if none of it is mine?

I was born in Germany. I didn't earn that. It was given.
Most of what I own came with it. We call that luck and move on.

But what if it's not luck.
What if it isn't yours. Even if it feels like it is.
What if at the end everything gets weighed — everyone by what he was given.
What if 90 times more means you're held responsible for 90 times more.
And what if the man you look down on — the one pulling the cart — is better than you.

Tahsin gave me his gloves and refused to take one back. I've seen it on four continents: the people who have the least give the most.

I don't rescue anyone. I sit with them and let them teach me.
I'm not saying it is. I'm just saying: what if.

Is it yours? Tell me.
