## SEGMENT 1 · 00:00:04 – 00:06:03 · Intro → the thesis → Jammeh named
Benjamin Q (re-record EN): "Who are you, how long have you been here — and how are you?" / later: "Where do they come from? Many from Gambia — there was this dictator, can you tell me?"
EN DUB (Kamara):
- I'm doing really good. I'm Kamara. I come from Guinea, Conakry. I'm here since 98.
- But my problem here in Germany — my brothers who are here, from my home, from all over Africa — they are not allowed to work.
- Sometimes I ask myself: why do they give us no chance to work? Or to study?
- Many boys here — they are not criminals. No. The government, the city makes them criminal. Because when a man has no work, you wait for somebody to feed you.
- Many here are strong. I see it in their faces. They really want to do something, to fight. But the government gives no chance.
- They get 300 every month. What do I do with 300? Better I go work — I don't care, 4 in the morning until 4 in the afternoon. Then I get over 1,000 euro.
- Look — many here, they were not dealers. No. But with this very small money, their friends say: man, these 300 — come, buy this, sell it. He does it once, twice — he says: oh, my friend is right. But it's bad.
- He buys bad things — for example drugs — and sells them. Maybe he makes over 500. Then he cannot say anymore: I go to school, I go to work. He continues.
- I'm a kindergarten teacher. I work with children. I ask many boys here: what do you do? He goes to school. When school is finished — there is no work. So he comes here.
- I tell many here: you are young, please fight for something. But when you look for work, they say: we need your work permit. And he doesn't get it. Why? Because I'm an asylum seeker? Okay, I understand — but I also need my life. Not this 300.
- Please make a good life for the people. Otherwise there will always be crime. Nobody in the whole world can go from morning to evening without drinking, without eating. If you have nothing, you go straight out on the street to do something bad. Believe me.
- I told my wife: no — they were not criminals. They became criminals here.  [EDITOR: THE line. Let it breathe. Cold-open candidate.]
- This dictator — Yahya Jammeh.

## SEGMENT 2 · 00:06:09 – 00:08:05 · Why they flee → house speech → gold
EN DUB:
- Look — in Africa, when there is dictatorship or civil war, nobody can stay there. You see it in Sudan. Or before — Liberia, Sierra Leone. In these wars many, many, many people died. Became hungry. Became violent.
- Many disappeared — to Europe, or to the neighbor countries. We are not here because we have no house at home. Everyone here — his father and his mama have their own house. We are not renters. No. We have our own houses. Only here we pay rent.
- My country is a rich country — but it makes me poor.
- Sometimes I ask myself: God — why does my country have gold, diamonds, petrol, oil, everything — but the people get nothing?

## SEGMENT 3 · 00:08:13 – 00:12:40 · Corruption → Nazi-video question → neighbor/"animal" → the lost → the comeback
Benjamin Q (re-record EN): "What do you say to people who judge? I saw a video of a Nazi. He comes into Görlitzer Park to provoke. People say don't film me — he says: this is my country, I film here. And he shows it like: look what Germany is. Many judge the people here without knowing anything. What do you say?"
EN DUB:
- Our problem is the government. A weak government. Corruption — without end.
- Yes — for sure, they think badly about us.
- I have a neighbor. She never once said good day to me. She thought maybe I came over Lampedusa.
- Then at a neighbors' meeting, somebody said: he works in the kindergarten, with children. He makes the children good for Germany. He makes our future. And now she wants to be nice. Now she says: hello, good day.
- I never say one word to her. Before, she thought of me like an animal. Where I come from, in Africa: if somebody thought of you as an animal — I will never become human for you. I stay an animal forever.  [EDITOR: emotional peak. DUB-TEST THIS FIRST.]
- Wallahi — if you are not bad yourself, if you don't steal, you don't suspect it in others. But if you are bad, you always think: he is exactly the same. If you have lost your life, you think: ah — he is lost too. But no. You are the one who lost. Not him.
- That is typical here. When they have lost their life, they think it's our fault — us, the foreigners.  [FLAG: broad statement about "their culture" — his words, his right; Benjamin decides if the "typical Germany, their culture" phrasing stays verbatim or as this softer sense-true version.]
- I told one: I studied here. What did you do? No training. Were you born here? I was not born here — I was 25 when I came. You were born here. Where is your education? Where is your certificate? Nothing. And then you tell me I am bad.

## SEGMENT 4 · 00:12:47 – 00:16:55 · Faith question → never sold → racism breaks me → the answer → 3 jobs
Benjamin Q (re-record EN): "You said wallahi — I'm Muslim too, I pray too. Many here are Muslims. They pray — and after, they sell. How do they live with this contradiction?"
EN DUB:
- My whole life here — I never sold 0.1 gram. No. I didn't come here alone. A German woman — she met me in my homeland. She brought me here.  [FLAG: wife line is identifying — his family's privacy. Benjamin decides. It also kills the cliché of how he came.]
- What I don't understand about living with Germany: this racism, this humiliation against foreigners. It breaks me. My children said it too — I have to go home. And I will go home anyway. No — I won't stay here. No.
- The ones here — they all pray. We all pray. We all do Ramadan, from day one until the end. But the problem is what I just said. When he thinks: how can I eat? What do you get to eat from the city? Nothing. Go home — no food, nothing at home. When he gets 50 euro here, he goes and buys food — you see a bag next to him. In the evening he goes home. Then he has food. That's why they do it here.
- If they had money, good work — you see nobody here. I know three boys who have good jobs now. They never come here anymore. One washes plates in a big restaurant — gets over 1,000 euro. One works in a warehouse. One is a janitor in a school. They don't come here anymore.
- Many told me: if I have work — what would I do here? What would I do here? They are not here for their life. No. Just to have a little — because they have no food at home.
